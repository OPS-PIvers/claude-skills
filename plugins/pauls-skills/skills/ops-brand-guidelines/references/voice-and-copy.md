# OPS voice and copy

This file sets how OPS copy reads, whoever writes it. It is the district's voice. When a piece goes out under Paul's name, his own voice profile applies on top of this.

OPS copy means the words in slides, newsletters, handouts, and web tools. The full list of machine-writing tells is in the `deslop` skill (`references/writing-ban-list.md`). Run `deslop --writing` on finished copy. This file names only the rules that come up most in OPS copy and the ones deslop doesn't cover.

## The test

Read the sentence out loud as a teacher talking to colleagues. If a competent person wouldn't say it that way, rewrite it.

## Sentences

**One thought per sentence.** When two halves could each stand alone, make them two sentences.

> **Before:** The form closes Friday, so make sure your team submits before then, and reach out if you have questions.
> **After:** The form closes Friday. Email Paul with questions.

The tell is two full clauses glued with ", and", ", so", or ", but". It often shows up right after an em dash gets removed. Fix it by splitting. A different glue word leaves the chain in place.

**Lead with the point.** The first sentence says what the reader needs to know or do. Background comes after, or gets cut.

**Name the thing.** Use the same noun every time. If it's the "sign-up form" in the first sentence, it isn't the "registration portal" in the third.

**Say what it does.**

> **Before:** The report can help provide insight into student engagement.
> **After:** The report shows which students missed the quiz.

**Banned in OPS copy** (deslop has the full list):

- Em dashes. Use a period, a comma, or parentheses.
- "Not X, but Y" and "more than an X, it's a Y." Say Y.
- Groups of three added for rhythm. List what's there.
- "This matters," "why this matters," "here's the thing," "the key is."
- AI vocabulary: delve, leverage, robust, seamless, empower, unlock, elevate, transform, journey, landscape, game-changer.
- Openers that announce the content: "In this session we'll explore," "Let's dive in," "Imagine a classroom where."
- Exclamation points in staff and parent copy. One is allowed in a Mode 2 student piece.
- Emoji in staff and parent copy.

## Headings and titles

**Sentence case everywhere.** Use it for headings, slide titles, buttons, labels, and table headers. Capitalize the first word and proper nouns only (Orono Middle School, SpartBoard, Google Classroom, Schoology).

**No colon subtitles.** "Exit tickets: a practical guide for busy teachers" becomes "Exit tickets" or a claim title.

**Name the topic plainly.** Questions ("What's next?"), "Understanding X," and "Unlocking X" are banned as headings.

## Slides

**Claim titles on content slides.** The title states the slide's point as a sentence.

| Topic label | Claim title |
|---|---|
| Exit tickets | Exit tickets take 3 minutes and show who's lost |
| Chromebook data | Chromebook repairs dropped 40% after the case swap |
| Next steps | Try one exit ticket before our March session |

Title slides, section dividers, agenda slides, and closing slides keep short labels.

**The title check.** Read only the slide titles, in order. They should tell the whole story of the session.

**One idea per slide.** If a slide has two claims, it's two slides. If two slides make the same claim, merge them.

**Bullets are fragments.** Keep them short and parallel, with no ending periods, at three to five per slide. If a bullet needs a full sentence, the slide probably needs fewer bullets.

**A short text slide is fine.** Don't add an icon, a stat block, or a callout to fill space. Add a chart when the point is a number, or a photo or screenshot when people need to see something.

**Numbers need a source.** Every statistic on a slide gets a small source line in the footer ("Source: OMS Skyward attendance, Fall 2026").

## Words inside web tools

**Buttons say what happens.** Label a button with a verb plus its object, such as "Save rubric," "Send to class," or "Download CSV." Never use "Submit," "Click here," "Learn more," or "Get started" on their own.

**Errors say how to fix it.** State what went wrong, then what to do. Leave out the apology and the "Oops."

> **Before:** Oops! Something went wrong. Please try again.
> **After:** The file is over 10 MB. Choose a smaller file or compress it.

**Empty states say what to do next.**

> **Before:** Nothing to see here!
> **After:** No quizzes yet. Create one from the library.

**Confirmations name the result.**

> **Before:** Success!
> **After:** Rubric saved to Grade 7 Science.

**Labels are nouns.**

> **Before:** Please enter the due date
> **After:** Due date

## Callouts

A callout opens with a bold lead word that says what kind of note it is. A reader who can't see the tint still gets the meaning.

- **Heads up:** The Wi-Fi password changes Monday.
- **Due Friday:** Submit your PD hours in Frontline.
- **Action required:** Re-enroll your devices before March 1.

Use a callout for one note the reader must not miss. Most pieces need one at most.
